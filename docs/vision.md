# Vision

Status: Accepted Phase 0 direction

## Purpose

bizOS helps a small business move work from agreement to payment without forcing its people to
become accountants or system administrators.

The product should answer three questions instantly:

1. What needs my attention?
2. What happens next?
3. Is the business waiting on me or someone else?

## Product promise

**Run the work. bizOS handles the paperwork.**

The system turns everyday business intent into reliable documents, approvals, payment records, and
customer-facing statements. It explains consequences in plain language, keeps the detailed controls
available when needed, and makes the safe path the easiest path.

## Ten-year direction

bizOS begins with the document-to-payment workflow and grows into connected modules for CRM,
projects, inventory, automation, and AI assistance. Growth must feel additive: a business sees only
the modules and complexity it has chosen.

The platform is successful when:

- a new owner can issue a correct document without training;
- a team member can see the next action without understanding the underlying accounting;
- a business can add countries, currencies, locations, and modules without migrating products;
- integrations and agents use the same governed API and permission checks as humans;
- operational scale does not weaken tenant isolation or auditability.

## Principles

1. Simplicity is a constraint, not decoration.
2. Workflows are the primary product model; documents are durable outcomes.
3. Plain language leads; formal accounting and legal terms remain available in context.
4. Correctness and traceability win over clever automation.
5. Human confirmation remains visible for high-impact actions.
6. Global capability is designed into primitives, not retrofitted into screens.
7. One deployable system is preferred until measured pressure justifies distribution.

## Measures

The north-star product measure is **median time from required action to correct completion**.
Supporting measures include first-document completion, correction rate, approval cycle time, payment
follow-up time, support contacts per active business, workflow abandonment, and accessibility
success.
